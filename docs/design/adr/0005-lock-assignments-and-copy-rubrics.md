# Lock assignments after submission and copy the rubric into each attempt

A teacher may find a mistake in an assignment after students have answered it. Editing the rubric in place would leave stored criteria, teacher reviews and class recap counts pointing at concepts the students never saw.

A posted assignment therefore locks when its first student submission arrives. Its teacher can unlock it, edit it and save, and saving locks it again. Each explanation attempt keeps a copy of the assignment's question, concepts and follow-ups from the moment it starts, and its results and reviews are always shown against that copy. The teacher's desk labels attempts made before the latest edit.

A concept whose text changes receives a new ID, so earlier evidence does not count toward the reworded concept in the class recap. A concept left unchanged keeps its ID and its evidence. A removed concept leaves the current recap and stays in learning history.

Full version history was rejected as more storage, recap and interface work than the hackathon allows. Free editing was rejected because it silently rewrites what students were assessed against, contrary to ADR 0001 and ADR 0002.
